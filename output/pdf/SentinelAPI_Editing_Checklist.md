# Wikipedia writing checklist

The [WikiProject AI Cleanup guide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing) compiles observations from Wikipedia. A pattern can occur in human writing, and its absence proves nothing about authorship.

- **Claims:** Inflated impact or influence; manufactured notability, media coverage, source recognition, or online prominence; shallow analysis; vague associations; sweeping attributions; formulaic outlook sections; filler awards.
- **Language:** Clustered stock vocabulary; roundabout substitutes for *is* and *are*; repeated contrast formulas; treating a descriptive title as a proper name; mechanically grouping ideas in threes.
- **Chat residue:** Reader-facing offers, drafting advice, source-access or publication hedges, fill-in wording, placeholder dates and URLs.
- **Citations:** Dead links; invalid identifiers; real DOIs pointing elsewhere; incomplete book citations; unconventional references; tracking parameters; search links; unused reference definitions.
- **Wikipedia process:** Generic edit summaries; boilerplate compliance claims; inventorying what was kept or avoided; overexplaining citations or template fields; narrating draft review; reviewer pitches; premature maintenance tags; templated profiles; rapid edits to build permissions.
- **Other context:** Abrupt voice changes, model/version differences, and biases in topic or language coverage.
- **Historical patterns:** Ad copy, knowledge-cutoff notices, canned recaps, pasted refusals, heavy dash use, truncated output, stale access dates, and forced synonym rotation.

The companion [style guide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing/Style) covers excessive capitalization and bold, repeated bold-label lists, redundant or skipped heading levels, emoji, unnecessary tables, and smart punctuation. The [markup guide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing/Markup) covers stray Markdown, broken wiki syntax, internal citation and highlighting tokens, and invented wiki categories or templates. The [comments guide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_use_in_comments) covers stock policy assurances and appeals, scripted requests for review, invented shortcuts, repetitive courtesies, unnecessary subject lines or sections, pasted chat logs, and formulaic disputes.

For this report, the useful checks were concrete claims, consistent vocabulary, fewer repeated explanations, readable headings, and usable references. Tables remain where readers need to compare results. Archive figures continue to describe the original test runs.
